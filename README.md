### **MSc Thesis (2025.9)**  On How Transformers Recognise Quantifier Depth in First-order logic
  - Transformers have attracted growing interest for their abilities and limitations in modelling formal languages. However, such studies do not adequately reflect real-world texts, where models must capture the structure amid lexical elements. In this work, we treat first-order logic (FOL) formulas as a combination of {a, b}∗ and Dyck-1 with predicates and variables, to better simulate the syntactic conditions of natural languages. Within this framework, we systematically study the abilities of Transformers to recognise quantifier depth (QD) as well as the internal mechanism of self-attention in doing so. Specifically, we define five FOL types with increasing structural complexity and provide stepwise, interpretable RASP algorithms that simulate the full computation of a Transformer encoder on processing these formulas. We then construct seven synthetic datasets covering the five FOL types and two FOL-to-Natural-Sentence (FOL2NS) translation variants, and evaluate (i) small-sized Transformers with different configurations on the FOL datasets and (ii) BERT and a two-layer, two-head (2L2H) Transformer on the FOL2NS datasets. Empirically, two-layer Transformers perform robustly among all datasets, whereas single-layer models degrade as the structure becomes more complex. Our attention-based analyses of the 2L2H model further support an “extractor → aggregator” progression across layers and reveal additional fine-grained patterns of QD recognition. Several attention heads show behaviours that either closely mirror the visualisations of our algorithms or align with observations previously reported for larger models. We also find shortcut learning in Transformer-based architectures when processing natural sentences in FOL2NS.

### **Seminar Project (2025.5)**  FOL2NS: Generating Natural Sentences from First-Order Logic
  - Translating formal language into natural language is a foundational challenge in NLP, driving various downstream applications in semantic parsing, theorem validation and question answering. In this study, I introduce First-order logic to Natural Sentence (FOL2NS), a neurosymbolic framework to provide synthetic FOL formulas as well as convert them into human expression. It covers a deeply nested structure with gradual quantifier depth (QD) that is rarely captured by existing corpora. By combining rule-driven modules with fine-tuning language models, FOL2NS extends the diversity and coverage of generated samples. In the experiments, I systematically evaluate the model’s ability with both character-level analysis and overall performance. Related results show that FOL2NS can reliably produce well-formed templates and fluent statements, but faces a challenge for more precise semantic representations and natural generation with increasing structural complexity.

### **Coursework Report1 (2025.5)**  From BERT to T5: A Study of Named Entity Recognition
  - Named entity recognition (NER) has been one of the essential pre-processing steps in modern NLP applications. This report focuses on implementing this downstream task through fine-tuning two pretrained models: (i) an encoder-only model (BERT) with a simple classification head, and (ii) a sequence-to-sequence model (T5) with few-shot prompts. Under the original 7-class tag and 3-class simplified tag schemes, the experiment applied a weighted cross-entropy for BERT and two validation strategies for T5. It also conducted an ablation study with different hyperparameters. Moreover, the related analysis provides valuable insights into common errors in BERT and the two models’ performance. Based on a bunch of performance metrics, this report aims to compare the above two architectures and explore their abilities in the sequence labelling task, laying the groundwork for further practical use cases.

### **Coursework Report2 (2025.1)**  On the Shoulders of Feature Preprocessing
  -  This report focuses on the design and implementation of a multifunctional classifier to efficiently process different classification tasks, including sentiment analysis, product type classification and helpfulness analysis. A softmax classifier with multinomial logistic regression was chosen as the foundational model. The classifier incorporates a modular design for task switching and flexible extensions of feature engineering by
dynamically controlling parameters. This modular system supports unigram and bigram tokenization, one-hot and Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency(TF-IDF) encoding, stopword filtering and their combinations, which facilitates an efficient and streamlined experimental process. In the experiments, the system identifies the most suitable model configurations for each task, offering an actionable insight into task-specific optimization on the shoulders of feature preprocessing.
